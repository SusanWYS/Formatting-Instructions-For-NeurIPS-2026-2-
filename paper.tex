\begin{abstract}
Do language models merely behave as if they infer implicit causes, or do they encode causal relationships that generalize? Four studies test behavioral elicitation, relation selectivity, syntactic transfer, and naturalistic transfer. Across six models, behavioral conclusions depend on elicitation method, whereas causal directions discriminate held-out causal pairs and reach .75--.94 accuracy after a cross-sentence to within-sentence shift. Demographic substitutions also shift causal projections in 65 matched scenarios per model. These studies connect pragmatic behavior to internal representation while exposing important construct-validity limits.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cohen and Kehler call defeasible pragmatic enrichments \emph{conversational elicitures} \citep{cohen-kehler-2021-conversational}. Language models reproduce human-like eliciture judgments and relative-clause preferences \citep{pan-kehler-2025-pragmatic}, but behavioral agreement does not reveal whether they encode the inferred relation. Because linear representations can reveal event-plausibility distinctions obscured by output probabilities \citep{lepori-etal-2026-fantasy}, we use contrastive activation addition (CAA) \citep{rimsky-etal-2024-steering} to study implicit causality.


We ask three questions: 
\begin{itemize}[label=, noitemsep, topsep=0pt, leftmargin=*]
\item
\textbf{RQ1} \textit{Do model behaviors reflect inferences about implicit causal relationships within sentences? }
\item \textbf{RQ2} \textit{Do LMs form internal representations of implicit causal relationships?}
\item \textbf{RQ3} \textit{If \textbf{RQ2} is true, do representations of implicit causality generalize to naturalistic settings with potential real-world implications?}
\end{itemize}

\section{Study 1: Measuring Behavioral Eliciture}

We vary the response measure in a conceptual replication of Pan and Kehler, finding substantial measurement dependence.

\subsection{Methods}

\textbf{Models.} We evaluate the base checkpoints of Gemma~2 2B/9B \citep{gemma-team-2024-gemma2}, Qwen3 4B/8B \citep{qwen-team-2025-qwen3}, Llama~3.2 3B \citep{meta-2024-llama32}, and Llama~3 8B \citep{dubey-etal-2024-llama3}.

\textbf{Dataset and analysis.} We evaluate 150 critical/control pairs with yes/no judgments, expected seven-point Likert scores, and the relative probability of the same continuation after \emph{because} versus bare \emph{and}. The last measure uses no metalinguistic prompt. A pair is correct when the critical item receives the stronger causal response, allowing us to compare prompting with probability measurement \citep{hu-levy-2023-prompting}.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=.9\linewidth]{figures/rq1_behavior.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{RQ1.} Paired accuracy on 150 eliciture pairs. Dashed line: chance. Behavioral conclusions vary substantially with response mapping, particularly for smaller models.}
  \label{fig:rq1}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Results}

Figure~\ref{fig:rq1} shows that Qwen3 4B/8B reach 1.00 with Likert responses and at least .96 with yes/no, but fall to .79/.76 under probability assignment. Conversely, Gemma~2 2B scores .45/.39 with prompting but .83 with \emph{because} versus \emph{and}. Overall, no interface supports a model-independent conclusion.

\section{Study 2: Relation-Selective Causal Representations}

We compare cross-sentence causal directions with temporal and plausibility directions, finding strong diagonals and weaker cross-transfer.

\subsection{Methods}

\textbf{Datasets.} Causal, temporal, and plausibility directions respectively use 384/96, 800/200, and 56/14 train/ID-development pairs.

\textbf{Analysis.} At every fourth layer and the final layer, CAA averages final-token differences between matched positive and negative texts. We select the earliest layer maximizing mean own-development paired accuracy and ROC--AUC, then apply that frozen direction to every target relation. Matrix diagonals measure within-relation discrimination; off-diagonals measure cross-transfer. This is an ID-development analysis, not an OOD test.

\subsection{Results}

Figures~\ref{fig:rq2-ct}--\ref{fig:rq2-cp} show causal self-transfer of \CausalSelfRange{}, temporal self-transfer of .93--.95, and plausibility self-transfer of .93--1.00. However, causal-to-temporal accuracy is .47--.73 and causal-to-plausibility is .36--.71; reverse transfer reaches .34--.67 and .15--.46. The gaps support relation selectivity, while occasional above-chance transfer argues against strict orthogonality.

\section{Study 3: Generalization Across Syntax}

We next test whether cross-sentence causal directions generalize to within-sentence eliciture.

\subsection{Methods}

We apply each Study~2 causal direction to 200 held-out within-sentence relative-clause pairs, changing both syntax and discourse unit \citep{rohde-etal-2011-anticipating}. A pair is correct when the critical sentence projects farther along the direction.

\section{Study 4: Generalization to Naturalistic Scenarios}

We finally test causal representations on matched naturalistic scenarios.

\subsection{Methods}

\textbf{Dataset.} We evaluate 65 self-blinding scenarios per model, with matched White-male, minority-male, and minority-female versions.

\textbf{Analysis.} A unit causal direction fit separately on 1,000 within-sentence pairs projects each scenario's final-token state. We report within-scenario differences from the White-male version with 10,000-sample bootstrap 95\% intervals.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=.9\linewidth]{figures/rq3_self_blinding.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{RQ3.} Mean paired CAA-projection change (65 scenarios/model); bootstrap 95\% intervals.}
  \label{fig:rq3}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Results}

Figure~\ref{fig:rq3} shows positive point estimates for both contrasts in every model. Minority-female intervals exclude zero for all six; minority-male intervals do so for four. Notably, minority-female magnitudes range from .06--.07 in Llama models to 2.25 in Gemma~2 9B. These associations do not establish social bias, downstream behavior, or a causal mechanism.

\section{Discussion}

Across four studies, behavioral evidence is measurement-sensitive, whereas causal directions are relation-selective and transferable. However, Study~2 uses ID development sets (only 14 plausibility pairs), CAA is correlational, and 65 scenarios cannot establish general social behavior. OOD tests, causal interventions, and behavioral validation remain necessary.
