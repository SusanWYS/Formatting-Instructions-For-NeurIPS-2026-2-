\subsection{Measuring Implicit Causal Inference through Model Behaviors}

\textbf{Models.} In this and all of the following experiments, we test Gemma~2 2B/9B \citep{gemma-team-2024-gemma2}, Qwen3 4B/8B \citep{qwen-team-2025-qwen3}, Llama~3.2 3B \citep{meta-2024-llama32}, and Llama~3 8B \citep{dubey-etal-2024-llama3} base checkpoints.

\textbf{Dataset.} We use 150 manually authored pairs of sentences. In each pair, the modifier or relative clause in the critical sentence provides a plausible cause of that outcome based on physical or everyday world knowledge; in the \emph{control} sentence, it provides an incidental detail. For example, \emph{The executive who falsified quarterly revenue figures was fired} is paired with \emph{The executive who wore a blue silk tie was fired}. This dataset extends the no-context eliciture paradigm of \citet{pan-kehler-2025-pragmatic}, which tests whether an apparently descriptive modifier is nevertheless understood as an explanation.

\textbf{Yes/No.} We first present the critical/control sentences as context and then ask, ``Does [modifier content] explain why [outcome]?'' We compute the probabilities of the complete answers \emph{Yes} and \emph{No}, including capitalization variants, and normalize them to obtain $P(\text{Yes})$.

\textbf{7-point Likert.} We first present the critical/control sentences as context and then ask how strongly the modifier explains the outcome on a scale from 1 (definitely not) through 4 (uncertain) to 7 (definitely yes). We compute a probability for each of the seven complete answers and use their probability-weighted mean as the model's rating.

\textbf{``I don't know why.''} Following \citet{pan-kehler-2025-pragmatic}, we remove the sentence-final period and append \emph{, and I don't know why.} We use the surprisal of this continuation as the score. The intuition is that if the modifier has already supplied an explanation, subsequently claiming \emph{I don't know why.} should be less expected and therefore more surprising. 

For all three measures, paired accuracy is the proportion of pairs in which the critical sentence scores strictly above its control; chance is $.5$, ties count as incorrect.

\subsection{Identifying Internal Representations of Implicit Causality Inference}

Pragmatic theories state that cross-sentence causal inferences should be much stronger, and are usually non-optional in human communication. This motivates RQ2: Do LMs form internal representations of implicit causal relationships across sentences? Specifically, we want to ask whether model activations distinguish causal relationships from other relations. If a representation is specific to causality, a causal direction should separate causal pairs well but transfer less reliably to temporal or plausibility pairs.

\textbf{Datasets.} 

\textbf{CAA directions.} Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) represents a relation with a direction in a model's activation space \citep{rimsky-etal-2024-steering}. At each tested layer, we subtract the final-token activation of every negative item from that of its matched positive item, then average these differences over the training pairs. An evaluation item receives a scalar score by projection onto this direction of averaged difference; paired accuracy is the proportion of evaluation pairs for which the positive item scores higher.
