\subsection{Study 1: Measuring Behavioral Eliciture}

An eliciture is a defeasible, unstated connection within an utterance \citep{cohen-kehler-2021-conversational}. We test causal elicitures with three measures.

\textbf{Methods. Models.} We test Gemma~2 2B/9B \citep{gemma-team-2024-gemma2}, Qwen3 4B/8B \citep{qwen-team-2025-qwen3}, Llama~3.2 3B \citep{meta-2024-llama32}, and Llama~3 8B \citep{dubey-etal-2024-llama3} base checkpoints.

\textbf{Dataset.} We adapt 150 matched within-sentence pairs from \citet{pan-kehler-2025-pragmatic}. Critical items contain a plausible cause; controls replace it with an incidental detail.

\textbf{Measures.} An item's score is (i) its probability of a yes answer, (ii) its expected seven-point rating, or (iii) its continuation probability after \emph{because} relative to bare \emph{and}; the last uses no question \citep{hu-levy-2023-prompting}. Paired accuracy is the proportion of critical items scoring above their controls.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=.72\linewidth]{figures/rq1_behavior.pdf}
  \caption{For \textbf{RQ1}, this experiment compares three measures of causal eliciture in six LMs. Bars show paired accuracy on 150 pairs with Wilson 95\% CIs; the dashed line marks chance. \textbf{Main takeaway: behavioral conclusions depend strongly on the measure.}}
  \label{fig:rq1}

\paragraph{Results.}

Figure~\ref{fig:rq1} shows that Qwen3 4B/8B reach 1.00 with Likert ratings and at least .96 with yes/no questions, but fall to .79/.76 with the probability measure. Gemma~2 2B shows the reverse pattern: .45/.39 with prompting and .83 with the probability measure. Thus, no single measurement method yields the same conclusion across models.

\subsection{Study 2: Relation-Selective Causal Representations}

We next ask whether model activations distinguish causal relationships from other relations. If a representation is specific to causality, a causal direction should separate causal pairs well but transfer less reliably to temporal or plausibility pairs.

\paragraph{Methods.}

\textbf{Datasets.} The causal data contain cross-sentence critical/control pairs in which the second sentence does or does not explain the first (384 training and 96 in-distribution evaluation pairs). The temporal data contrast events that are likely or unlikely to occur during a stated activity (800/200 pairs). The plausibility data contrast probable and impossible events (56/14 pairs).

\textbf{CAA directions.} Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) represents a relation with a direction in a model's activation space \citep{rimsky-etal-2024-steering}. At each tested layer, we subtract the final-token activation of every negative item from that of its matched positive item, then average these differences over the training pairs. A new text receives a scalar score by projection onto this direction; paired accuracy is the proportion of evaluation pairs for which the positive item scores higher.

\textbf{Layer selection and transfer.} We test every fourth layer and the final layer. For each relation, we select the earliest layer that maximizes the mean of same-relation paired accuracy and ROC--AUC, freeze that direction, and evaluate it on every target relation. These are in-distribution evaluation sets used for model selection, not held-out OOD tests.

\paragraph{Results.}

Figures~\ref{fig:rq2-ct}--\ref{fig:rq2-cp} show causal same-relation accuracy of \CausalSelfRange{}, temporal accuracy of .93--.95, and plausibility accuracy of .93--1.00. Cross-transfer is lower: causal-to-temporal accuracy is .47--.73 and causal-to-plausibility accuracy is .36--.71; the reverse directions reach .34--.67 and .15--.46. The large diagonal--off-diagonal gaps support relation selectivity, while occasional above-chance transfer shows that the directions are not strictly independent.

\subsection{Study 3: Generalization to Causal OOD}

Study~2 measures in-distribution separation. We now ask whether the learned directions remain informative on unseen causal scenarios.

\paragraph{Methods.}

\textbf{Training and evaluation data.} We fit the causal direction on 1,000 within-sentence critical/control pairs and select its layer on 200 development pairs. The temporal direction uses 800/200 training/development pairs. Because only 70 probable/impossible pairs are available, the plausibility direction uses five-fold layer selection. We then evaluate all three frozen directions, without further fitting, on the same 150 unseen causal critical/control pairs.

\textbf{Analysis.} We project the final-token activation of each sentence onto a direction. A pair is correct when its causally related critical sentence projects farther than its matched control. We report paired accuracy with Wilson 95\% confidence intervals (CIs).

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=.78\linewidth]{figures/rq2b_causal_ood_caa_accuracy.pdf}
  \caption{For \textbf{RQ2b}, this experiment compares causal, temporal, and plausibility CAA directions on the same 150 unseen causal pairs. Bars show paired accuracy with Wilson 95\% CIs; the dashed line marks chance. \textbf{Main takeaway: the causal direction generalizes most consistently, indicating that its performance is not explained by temporal or plausibility information alone.}}
  \label{fig:rq2b}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Results.}

Figure~\ref{fig:rq2b} shows that the causal direction reaches .97--1.00 paired accuracy across models. The temporal direction varies from .57--.95, whereas the plausibility direction ranges from .41--.69. Thus, causal information generalizes consistently; information carried by the comparison directions is weaker and more model-dependent.

\subsection{Study 4: Generalization to Naturalistic Scenarios}

We finally test whether causal projections change when demographic descriptors vary in otherwise matched naturalistic sentences.

\paragraph{Methods.}

\textbf{Dataset.} We use 65 scenarios describing an adverse decision or outcome. Each scenario has three versions that differ only in demographic wording: White male, minority male, and minority female. Minority descriptors are Black, Hispanic, or Asian, and the same race is used in the two minority versions of each scenario.

\textbf{Analysis.} For each model, we use the causal direction fit on the 1,000 within-sentence training pairs in Study~3 and normalize it to unit length. We project each scenario's final-token activation onto this direction, then subtract the White-male score from each matched minority score. A positive difference means that the minority version aligns more strongly with the learned causal direction. We report mean differences with 10,000-sample bootstrap 95\% CIs over scenarios.

\paragraph{Results.}

Table~\ref{tab:rq3} shows positive mean differences for both contrasts in every model. The minority-female CI excludes zero for all six models; the minority-male CI excludes zero for four. Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:rq3-distributions} nevertheless shows substantial overlap among the raw score distributions. The estimates therefore identify representational associations, not evidence of social bias in model behavior or of a causal mechanism.

\section{Discussion}

Behavioral evidence is measurement-sensitive, whereas causal directions are relation-selective and generalize to causal OOD data. However, Study~2 uses ID development sets (only 14 plausibility pairs), CAA is correlational, and 65 scenarios cannot establish general behavior. Larger tests, interventions, and behavioral validation remain necessary.
