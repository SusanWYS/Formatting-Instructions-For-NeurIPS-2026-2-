\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Study 1: Measuring Behavioral Eliciture}

We vary the response measure in a conceptual replication of Pan and Kehler, finding substantial measurement dependence.

\textbf{Models.} We evaluate the base checkpoints of Gemma~2 2B/9B \citep{gemma-team-2024-gemma2}, Qwen3 4B/8B \citep{qwen-team-2025-qwen3}, Llama~3.2 3B \citep{meta-2024-llama32}, and Llama~3 8B \citep{dubey-etal-2024-llama3}.

\textbf{Dataset and analysis.} We evaluate 150 critical/control pairs with yes/no judgments, expected seven-point Likert scores, and the relative probability of the same continuation after \emph{because} versus bare \emph{and}. The last measure uses no metalinguistic prompt. A pair is correct when the critical item receives the stronger causal response, allowing us to compare prompting with probability measurement \citep{hu-levy-2023-prompting}.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=.78\linewidth]{figures/rq1_behavior.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{RQ1.} Paired accuracy on 150 eliciture pairs with Wilson 95\% CIs. Dashed line: chance.}
  \label{fig:rq1}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Results.}

Figure~\ref{fig:rq1} shows that Qwen3 4B/8B reach 1.00 with Likert responses and at least .96 with yes/no, but fall to .79/.76 under probability assignment. Conversely, Gemma~2 2B scores .45/.39 with prompting but .83 with \emph{because} versus \emph{and}. Overall, no interface supports a model-independent conclusion.

\subsection{Study 2: Relation-Selective Causal Representations}

We compare cross-sentence causal directions with temporal and plausibility directions, finding strong diagonals and weaker cross-transfer.

\textbf{Datasets.} Causal, temporal, and plausibility directions respectively use 384/96, 800/200, and 56/14 train/ID-development pairs.

\textbf{Analysis.} At every fourth layer and the final layer, CAA averages final-token differences between matched positive and negative texts. We select the earliest layer maximizing mean own-development paired accuracy and ROC--AUC, then apply that frozen direction to every target relation. Matrix diagonals measure within-relation discrimination; off-diagonals measure cross-transfer. This is an ID-development analysis, not an OOD test.

\paragraph{Results.}

Figures~\ref{fig:rq2-ct}--\ref{fig:rq2-cp} show causal self-transfer of \CausalSelfRange{}, temporal self-transfer of .93--.95, and plausibility self-transfer of .93--1.00. However, causal-to-temporal accuracy is .47--.73 and causal-to-plausibility is .36--.71; reverse transfer reaches .34--.67 and .15--.46. The gaps support relation selectivity, while occasional above-chance transfer argues against strict orthogonality.

\subsection{Study 3: Generalization to Causal OOD}

We next test whether causal directions remain selectively informative on unseen causal scenarios.

\textbf{Datasets.} Causal and temporal CAAs use 1,000/200 and 800/200 train/development pairs; the plausibility CAA uses 70 probable/impossible pairs with five-fold layer selection. We evaluate every frozen direction on the same 150-pair causal OOD set.

\textbf{Analysis.} We project each critical/control sentence onto the corresponding direction. A pair is correct when the critical item projects farther than its matched control.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=.78\linewidth]{figures/rq2b_causal_ood_caa_accuracy.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{RQ2b.} Paired accuracy on 150 causal OOD pairs with Wilson 95\% CIs. Dashed line: chance.}
  \label{fig:rq2b}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Results.}

Figure~\ref{fig:rq2b} shows that causal CAA reaches .97--1.00 paired accuracy across models. Temporal CAA varies from .57--.95, whereas plausibility CAA ranges from .41--.69. The causal direction therefore generalizes most consistently to new causal scenarios; transfer from the comparison directions is weaker and model-dependent.

\subsection{Study 4: Generalization to Naturalistic Scenarios}

We finally test causal representations on matched naturalistic scenarios.

\paragraph{Methods.}

\textbf{Dataset.} We evaluate 65 self-blinding scenarios per model in matched White-male, minority-male, and minority-female versions.

\textbf{Analysis.} A unit causal direction fit separately on 1,000 within-sentence pairs projects each scenario's final-token state. We report within-scenario differences from the White-male version with 10,000-sample bootstrap 95\% intervals and visualize raw, unnormalized CAA projections.

\paragraph{Results.}

Table~\ref{tab:rq3} shows positive estimates for both contrasts in every model. Minority-female intervals exclude zero for all six; minority-male intervals do so for four. However, Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:rq3-distributions} shows substantial distributional overlap. These associations do not establish social bias, behavior, or a causal mechanism.

\section{Discussion}

Behavioral evidence is measurement-sensitive, whereas causal directions are relation-selective and generalize to causal OOD data. However, Study~2 uses ID development sets (only 14 plausibility pairs), CAA is correlational, and 65 scenarios cannot establish general behavior. Larger tests, interventions, and behavioral validation remain necessary.
