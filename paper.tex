\subsection{Identifying Internal Representations of Implicit Causality Inference}

We next ask whether model activations distinguish causal relationships from other relations. If a representation is specific to causality, a causal direction should separate causal pairs well but transfer less reliably to temporal or plausibility pairs.

\textbf{Datasets.} 

\textbf{CAA directions.} Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) represents a relation with a direction in a model's activation space \citep{rimsky-etal-2024-steering}. At each tested layer, we subtract the final-token activation of every negative item from that of its matched positive item, then average these differences over the training pairs. An evaluation item receives a scalar score by projection onto this direction of averaged difference; paired accuracy is the proportion of evaluation pairs for which the positive item scores higher.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \IfFileExists{figures/rq2a_causal_temporal_heatmaps.pdf}{\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/rq2a_causal_temporal_heatmaps.pdf}}{\rule{\linewidth}{.23\linewidth}}
  \caption{Cross-transfer paired accuracy between causal and temporal CAA directions. Rows show the CAA training set and columns show the in-distribution evaluation set. \textbf{Causal and temporal CAA directions are largely relation-selective.} Both directions perform best on their own relation and transfer less consistently to the other relation.}
