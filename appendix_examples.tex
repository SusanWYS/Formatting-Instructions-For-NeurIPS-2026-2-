\subsection{Behavioral Test: Canonical Causal Items}
\label{app:behavior-examples}

The ten canonical behavioral items contrast a causal-likely condition
($C_L$) with a causal-unlikely condition ($C_U$). The neutral condition from
the source dataset is omitted here for compactness.

1 & A pregnant CEO was fired this morning. & A CEO named Jack was fired this morning. \\
2 & A student desperate to boost their GPA was caught cheating on an exam. & A student with brown eyes was caught cheating on an exam. \\
3 & A paper by a stressed graduate student was found to contain fabricated data. & A paper by a scientist from Vermont was found to contain fabricated data. \\
4 & The guests staying near the elevator tossed and turned all night. & The guests with matching bags tossed and turned all night. \\
5 & A singer with a full rehearsal schedule got an angry call from their neighbor. & A retired singer got an angry call from their neighbor. \\
6 & The employee who hated their manager was caught embezzling funds. & An employee in the London office was caught embezzling funds. \\

8 & A forgery artist received a lawsuit. & A French artist received a lawsuit. \\
9 & A soccer player who hit a referee received a suspension. & A soccer player who scored two goals received a suspension. \\
10 & A resident who stopped paying rent was evicted this morning. & A resident on the third floor was evicted this morning. \\

\subsection{Within-Sentence Causal Items}
\label{app:within-sentence-examples}

set. Critical and control members hold the outcome and construction fixed while
changing whether the modifier plausibly explains the outcome.

\bottomrule
\endfoot
1 & The executive who falsified quarterly revenue figures was fired. & The executive who wore a blue silk tie was fired. \\
2 & The contractor repeatedly failing mandatory safety inspections lost the municipal contract. & The contractor carrying a blue clipboard with a metal clip lost the municipal contract. \\
3 & The department manager who altered employee performance records was demoted. & The department manager whose office was beside the west stairwell was demoted. \\
4 & The security officer implicated in disabling cameras to conceal a theft was dismissed. & The security officer shown in a square badge photograph was dismissed. \\
5 & The procurement director who accepted kickbacks from a supplier was removed. & The procurement director who commuted on the early morning train was removed. \\
6 & The accountant caught backdating tax records after an audit began was formally disciplined. & The accountant drinking coffee from a ceramic mug was formally disciplined. \\
7 & The supplier that bribed an official on the selection committee was barred from future bids. & The supplier whose brochure was printed on matte paper was barred from future bids. \\
8 & The clinic administrator implicated by an audit exposing diverted patient fees resigned. & The clinic administrator seated at a desk beside a narrow window resigned. \\
9 & The warehouse supervisor who ordered workers to bypass machine safety locks was suspended. & The warehouse supervisor who kept spare gloves in a lower drawer was suspended. \\
10 & The board member responsible for disclosing confidential board votes was expelled. & The board member taking notes with a black fountain pen was expelled. \\
